NVIDIA announced the Jetson Orin Nano Developer Kit on [21 March 2023](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/announcing-jetson-orin-nano-developer-kit/246900) at $499, with shipping planned for April. The board in that box is the board on sale now. In December 2024 NVIDIA kept the hardware, raised the clocks through a new power mode, renamed the kit Super, and cut the price to [$249](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-developer-kit-gets-a-super-boost/). In July 2026 the list price went to [$399](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/07/22/nvidia-increases-the-price-of-jetson-modules-and-devkits-by-up-to-101/). The new figure appeared in NVIDIA's [developer FAQ](https://developer.nvidia.com/embedded/faq). There was no announcement.

[Robu](https://robu.in/product/nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-super-developer-kit/) and [AI Roboo](https://airoboo.in/product/nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-super-developer-kit/) both showed the kit at ₹34,679 on 11 November 2026. Against the new $399 list, that is about ₹87 to the dollar. A Pi 5 8GB at $175 and ₹16,950 works out to about ₹97 to the dollar, so the rupee price is in the same range as the July increase. What we could not confirm is stock. Robu's category page linked to the product instead of showing an add-to-cart button. Check that a shop can ship one before you pay.

# Our verdict

Buy the developer kit if the project needs CUDA, TensorRT, or a camera decoded in hardware while a model runs. At the listed Indian price it costs about twice a [Pi 5 8GB](/review/raspberry-pi-5), which was ₹16,950 at Silverline the same day, and it does work the Pi does slowly.

Leave it if you want a home server, a desktop, or Pi-hole. The Pi 5 is the cheaper board for that, with a normal Debian system and lower idle draw. Leave the bare 4GB module too. It has half the GPU, and a carrier pushes the total up to the 8GB kit.

If you already own an original Orin Nano developer kit, keep it. The Super clocks are a software update.

**What we like**

- CUDA, TensorRT and DeepStream, the stack NVIDIA uses on its larger GPUs.
- Hardware decode for H.265, H.264, AV1 and VP9, so a camera feed does not land on the CPU.
- PCIe 3.0 x4 for an NVMe drive, plus a second M.2 slot at x2. A Pi 5 has one PCIe 2.0 lane.
- The 19V supply, the heatsink and fan, and a wireless module all come in the box.

**What we don't**

- The US price is $399, 60 percent above December 2024. We could not confirm either Indian shop had one ready to ship at ₹34,679.
- No hardware video encoder. NVIDIA rates 1080p30 encoding as work for one or two CPU cores.
- DisplayPort only. The USB-C port carries data, and a TV needs an adapter.
- NVIDIA's own FAQ says the developer kit is for development and test. The module in the box is not the part you ship in a product.
- 8GB is shared by the CPU and the GPU. NVIDIA's own table tops out at an 8B model.

# What's on the board

The [quick start guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/orin-nano-devkit/user-guide/latest/quick_start.html) lists three things in the box: the developer kit, a 19V supply, and a support card. The [hardware layout](https://docs.nvidia.com/jetson/orin-nano-devkit/user-guide/latest/hardware_layout.html) puts storage on a microSD slot on the module, or on an NVMe drive. Around the edge are four USB 3.2 ports at 10Gbps, gigabit Ethernet, DisplayPort, and a USB-C port that does not output video. Two 22-pin CSI connectors take cameras. Under the module there is an M.2 Key M slot for a 2280 drive at PCIe 3.0 x4, a second Key M slot for a 2230 drive at x2, and a Key E slot that ships with a wireless module fitted. The power jack is a 5.5mm by 2.5mm barrel.

You bring your own storage. From JetPack 7 there is no SD card image. The installer goes on a USB stick and flashes a microSD card or an NVMe drive, and the board's UEFI firmware has to be version 36.0 or newer before that installer will run. An older kit needs the JetPack 6 update path first. NVIDIA documents both steps on the [JetPack 7.2.1 downloads page](https://developer.nvidia.com/embedded/jetpack/downloads) and in the [7.2 getting-started thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/jetpack-7-2-jetson-linux-r39-2-on-jetson-orin-nano-developer-kit-getting-started-and-feedback-thread/372151).

A monitor has to take DisplayPort, or you need a DisplayPort-to-HDMI cable. NVIDIA says passive and active adapters both work. A Raspberry Pi Camera Module v2 needs a 15-pin to 22-pin cable. CAM0 is two CSI lanes. CAM1 can be two lanes or four. The 40-pin header is for prototyping, and its voltages are in the carrier specification rather than in the Pi header layout, so read that sheet before you plan around a Pi HAT.

The silicon under the heatsink did not change in 2024. NVIDIA's [Super post](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-jetson-orin-nano-developer-kit-gets-a-super-boost/) puts the old rating at 40 sparse INT8 TOPS, 68GB/s of memory, a 635MHz GPU and a 1.5GHz CPU. Super mode moves that to 67 sparse TOPS (33 dense), 102GB/s, 1,020MHz and 1.7GHz, and adds a 25W power budget next to the old 7W and 15W modes. Any original developer kit can take the same mode with a JetPack update.

The carrier was designed for more than this one module. At launch, NVIDIA said it accepts Orin Nano and Orin NX modules, so a later prototype can move to an NX without a new board. The NX is the one with a hardware video encoder.

An independent run of the same 3B model lands much lower. [PicoCluster](https://picocluster-claw.com/reference/benchmarks/) tested a board they identify as an Orin Nano Super 8GB, on JetPack 6.2 with llama.cpp, on 2 April 2026. Llama 3.2 3B at Q4_K_M generated 18.4 tokens per second in MAXN mode, 17.2 in the 25W mode, and 13.3 in the 15W mode, for a 128-token generation after a 512-token prompt. That is under half of NVIDIA's MLC number. Quote the figure for the runtime you will use.

On a Pi 5, [CNX Software](https://www.cnx-software.com/2026/01/20/raspberry-pi-ai-hat-2-review-a-40-tops-ai-accelerator-tested-with-computer-vision-llm-and-vlm-workloads/) measured Llama 3.2 3B at 4.78 tokens per second on the CPU. PicoCluster's lower Jetson figure is still about four times that, and it is running on CUDA. The Hailo chip on Raspberry Pi's AI HAT+ 2 was slower than the Pi's own CPU on the model CNX tried, and the HAT was ₹21,400 at Silverline. A Pi 5 8GB plus that HAT is ₹38,350, close to the Jetson's listed ₹34,679, and it runs a much shorter list of models.

8GB of shared memory is the ceiling NVIDIA itself describes. The Super post talks about models up to about 8B parameters, and Llama 3.1 8B at 19 tokens per second is the top row of their table. A 13B model has to share that 8GB with the operating system and the context.

The 1,020MHz GPU clock is the MAXN mode, not the 25W mode. PicoCluster measured 1,011 to 1,019MHz in MAXN, and about 910MHz with the 25W cap. In their logs the 25W mode also clocked the CPU lower than the 15W mode, 1,344MHz against 1,497MHz, and spent the extra budget on the GPU. On JetPack 6.1, NVIDIA's step for the top mode was `sudo nvpmodel -m 2`. The JetPack 7.2.1 installer flashes the Super configuration by default, so check the power-mode control on a new install instead of trusting the old mode number.

# Video

Decode is a separate block of silicon. NVIDIA's [module datasheet](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/5380/Jetson_Orin_Nano_Series_DS-11105-001_v1.1.pdf) (DS-11105-001) lists a multi-standard decoder for H.265, H.264, VP9, AV1, MPEG-4, MPEG-2 and VC-1. The stream counts in that sheet, which predates the Super clocks, are one 4K60 or eleven 1080p30 in H.265, and one 4K30 or seven 1080p30 in H.264. Super mode does not add a decoder. It raises the clocks around the one already on the chip. DeepStream 9.1 is on the supported list for JetPack 7.2.1.

Encoding is software, as it is on the Pi 5. The Orin Nano has no NVENC block. The datasheet, and the [later module sheet](https://static.generation-robots.com/media/jetson-orin-datasheet-nano-modules.pdf) that carries the Super clocks, both say 1080p30 encoding runs on one or two CPU cores in software. Orin NX and AGX Orin have a hardware encoder. A product that has to record or restream compressed video all day wants one of those, not this module. Decoding a camera into a detector is the job the Nano is built for.

# Software

The current release is [JetPack 7.2.1](https://developer.nvidia.com/embedded/jetpack/downloads): Jetson Linux 39.2.1, Ubuntu 24.04, kernel 6.8, CUDA 13.2.2 and TensorRT 10.16.2. JetPack 7.2, the release before it, is when Orin joined the JetPack 7 line. NVIDIA's download page lists Isaac ROS as still coming soon, so a robotics project that needs Isaac may be better off staying on JetPack 6, which remains the sustaining release for Orin. Canonical's Ubuntu 24.04 for Jetson is listed alongside Jetson Linux.

A Pi boots Raspberry Pi OS, Ubuntu or Home Assistant OS from an ordinary image. A Jetson boots the image NVIDIA built for that JetPack.

NVIDIA's FAQ says a developer kit is a non-production module on a reference carrier, used to develop and test, and that a production module ships with no software on it. You flash it with the image you have already proved out. The Orin family stays in production through 2032, which is the commitment NVIDIA made in the Super announcement.

# Heat and power

The labelled modes are 7W, 15W and 25W. MAXN sits above the 25W cap. Those numbers are module budgets, not watts at the wall.

PicoCluster's 2 April 2026 run measured module input (VDD_IN) on a headless Super 8GB, with no USB devices, no monitor and no cameras. Idle was 5.2W in the 15W mode, 5.5W in the 25W mode, and 9.1W in MAXN. Llama 3.2 3B generation averaged 11.2W, 13.4W and 15.0W in those three modes. A five-minute soak with the CPU, GPU, NVMe and network all busy peaked at 71°C at the junction and settled at 67 to 68°C. The GPU clock stayed near 1,013MHz, and they recorded no throttling.

A board with a monitor and a camera will draw more, and a meter at the wall will read higher again because of the 19V adapter. Held at the 5.5W module figure, a year of idling is about 50 units of electricity, near ₹400 at ₹8 a unit, before the adapter. A Pi 5 idles around 2.8W at the wall in Jeff Geerling's test, linked from our Pi 5 review. We have no figure for a 35°C room, or for the board shut inside a case. The heatsink and fan are part of the kit.

# Which one to buy

**Developer kit, listed at ₹34,679.** The board to buy when the software you are writing needs CUDA or a hardware decoder, and when a shop will sell it near that price. Add a microSD card or an NVMe drive, and a DisplayPort cable.

**A kit you already own.** Install JetPack 7, or stay on JetPack 6 if the project needs Isaac ROS. Super mode is a power setting.

**8GB module.** For a product, after the software works and someone has designed the carrier, the case and the supply. A poor way to save money on a first board.

**4GB module.** Half the GPU. Once a carrier is in the basket, the total sits next to the 8GB kit.

**Pi 5, with or without an AI HAT.** For GPIO work, Home Assistant, and anything that never calls CUDA. The HAT is the cheaper vision accelerator, and it will not run the TensorRT code a Jetson project is usually written in.
